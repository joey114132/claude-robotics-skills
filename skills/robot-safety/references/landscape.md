# Robot Safety & Compliance Landscape — verified snapshot
**Verified: 2026-10-02 (changed entries and link fixes; rest 2026-08-05).** Entries were confirmed against live sources on this date — re-verify anything before relying on it if this snapshot is more than ~6 months old.

## Robot-specific safety standards
- **ISO 10218-1:2025** — Robotics — Safety requirements — Part 1: Industrial robots (duties of the robot manufacturer); on sale from February 2025. Where the 2011 edition required a handful of safety functions the 2025 edition requires more than twenty, adds new robot classifications with matching functional-safety requirements and test methods, and adds cybersecurity requirements as they bear on robot safety. The 2011 edition is to be withdrawn in spring 2027, so files citing it have a deadline. Functional-safety levels now come from a normative Annex C table that differs by robot class: Class II needs PL d or SIL 2, while Class I (manipulator mass <= 10 kg, maximum force <= 50 N, speed <= 250 mm/s) needs PL b or SIL 1. The 2011 edition required PL d / Category 3 for the safety-related control functions. KEBA's summary of the new minimum is PL d / Cat 3, or SIL 2 / HFT=1 (20 years), or SIL 2 / PFHd < 4.43*10^-7, so Category 3 is no longer mandatory when a low enough PFHd is shown. Scope excludes "mobility when robots or manipulators are fixed to or part of driverless industrial trucks". Status: maintained. Source: https://www.therobotreport.com/iso-10218-industrial-robot-safety-standard-receives-major-overhaul/ and https://www.universal-robots.com/blog/safer-clearer-and-more-explicit-iso-10218-gets-a-makeover/ (overview), https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17822 (Class I/II limits, Annex C, PL/SIL by class), https://www.keba.com/en/news/industrial-automation/interview-robotics-standard (PL d / SIL 2 minimums and PFHd), https://genorma.com/en/standards/iso-10218-1-2025 (scope exclusion wording)
- **ISO 10218-2:2025** — Part 2: Industrial robot applications and robot cells (duties of the integrator); on sale from February 2025. Absorbs most of the collaborative-application content formerly in ISO/TS 15066 and the end-effector guidance formerly in ISO/TR 20218; treats collaboration as a property of the *application*, not the robot. Status: maintained. Source: https://theresarobotforthat.com/blog/iso-10218-2025-explained/
- **ISO/TS 15066:2016** — Collaborative robots technical specification; the four collaborative modes and the biomechanical contact-limit tables. Its content is now normatively integrated into ISO 10218:2025 rather than cited standalone. Status: superseded in practice — verify before citing. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17822
- **ANSI/A3 R15.06-2025** — US national adoption of ISO 10218-1:2025 and -2:2025 in full, plus a US/Canada-developed Part 3 on use of industrial robot cells. Parts 1-2 approved 21 Aug 2025, Part 3 approved 7 Oct 2025, published 29 Oct 2025; 403 pages; replaces ANSI/RIA R15.06-2012. Carries ISO 10218-1:2025's term "monitored standstill" (2011: "safety-rated monitored stop"). Status: maintained. Source: https://www.therobotreport.com/now-available-full-403-page-ansi-a3-r15-06-2025-robot-safety-standard/ (dates, page count, replacement) and https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17822 (term change: 2011 clause 3.19.6 versus 2025 clause 3.1.8.4)

## Machinery functional-safety foundations (Type A/B standards)
- **ISO 12100:2010** — Safety of machinery: general principles for design, risk assessment and risk reduction. The root method every robot risk assessment inherits; still the published edition, but a second edition is in revision. ISO/DIS 12100.3 was last listed on 15 September 2026 at stage 40.60 "Close of voting", after the first draft (published 1 January 2025 as prEN ISO 12100:2024) was not approved and the second draft (15 November 2025) was rejected; a third draft followed on 1 August 2026. The new edition would cancel and replace ISO 12100:2010, ISO/TR 22100-1:2021 and ISO/TR 22100-2:2013, adds a reference to self-evolving behaviour of applied artificial intelligence (5.4.3) and adds a cybersecurity definition. Chinese counterpart GB/T 15706-2012 is current (issued 5 Nov 2012, effective 1 Mar 2013). Status: maintained, in revision. Source: https://www.ibf-solutions.com/en/seminars-and-news/news/new-en-iso-12100 (draft timeline, stage 40.60) and https://www.certifico.com/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=26621&catid=358 (what it replaces, AI and cybersecurity changes) and https://www.ndls.org.cn/standard/detail/fbdab6808e083e1e0131326e077b0b0e (GB/T 15706-2012 status and dates)
- **ISO 13849-1:2023** — Safety-related parts of control systems, Performance Levels PL a-e. EN version cited in the EU Official Journal May 2024 with a transition period to 15 May 2027; changes the methodology for determining the required level and adds application-software requirements by complexity. Status: maintained. Source: https://www.ibf-solutions.com/en/seminars-and-news/news/new-en-iso-13849-12023 (Official Journal publication 15 May 2024; presumption of conformity for the 2015 edition ends 15 May 2027) and https://machinerysafety101.com/2023/05/01/do-not-use-iso-13849-1-2023/ (a merged single-part ISO 13849-1 is projected for possibly 2028)
- **IEC 62061:2021 (+AMD1:2024)** — Functional safety of safety-related control systems using SIL 1-3; scoped to electrical/electronic/programmable-electronic systems, harmonised alongside ISO 13849-1. Status: maintained. Source: https://61508.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/09A-T6A-Symposium-Presentation-IEC-62061-and-ISO-13849.pdf
- **IEC 61508 Edition 2.0 (2010)** — the generic functional-safety umbrella for E/E/PE safety-related systems; the parent from which SIL derives and the fallback where no sector standard exists. Status: maintained. Source: https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/5515
- **ISO 13855:2024** — Positioning of safeguards with respect to the approach of the human body; published November 2024, replacing the now-withdrawn ISO 13855:2010. Sets the separation distances for electro-sensitive protective equipment, pressure-sensitive devices, two-hand controls and interlocking guards, for persons aged 14 and over. Status: maintained. Source: https://genorma.com/en/standards/iso-13855-2024 (iso.org/standard/80590.html blocks automated fetches)
- **IEC 61800-5-2** — Functional safety for power drive systems; defines the drive-level safety functions used in robot joints and mobile drivetrains: STO (Safe Torque Off), SS1 (Safe Stop 1), SLS (Safe Limited Speed), SLP (Safe Limited Position). Status: maintained. Source: https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/24556

## Mobile and service robot safety standards
- **ISO 3691-4:2023** — Industrial trucks — safety requirements — Part 4: driverless industrial trucks and their systems. Covers AGVs, AMRs, tuggers and carts; normative Annex A on preparing operating zones, Annex B hazard catalogue. A revision is in draft as ISO/DIS 3691-4: the DIS ballot has closed and the project is at stage 40.60 "Close of voting" (stage date 10 September 2026, per genorma's mirror of the ISO listing), so check the edition before quoting a clause. Status: maintained, in revision. Source: https://www.iso.org/standard/83545.html and https://genorma.com/en/standards/iso-dis-3691-4 (DIS stage and stage date; iso.org blocks automated fetches)
- **ANSI/A3 R15.08-1-2020** — US safety requirements for industrial mobile robots (IMRs), Part 1: the IMR itself; published December 2020; introduces the IMR Type A/B/C classification (Type A = mobile platform only). Status: maintained. Source: https://www.automate.org/robotics/industry-insights/mobile-robot-standard-r15-08-1-2020-what-you-need-to-know
- **ANSI/A3 R15.08-2-2023** — Part 2: requirements for IMR systems and IMR applications — integrating, configuring and customising a robot or fleet into a site. Status: maintained. Source: https://webstore.ansi.org/standards/ria/ansia3r15082023
- **ANSI/A3 R15.08-3-2026** — American National Standard for Industrial Mobile Robots, Safety Requirements, Part 3: Use of IMR Applications; announced 29-30 September 2026, completing the three-part R15.08 series. Focuses on the responsibilities of users operating IMR applications in the workplace: requirements and guidance for maintaining acceptable risk through day-to-day operations and the machinery lifecycle, including risk assessment, management of change, employee training, and periodic reviews and inspections. Status: maintained. Source: https://www.roboticstomorrow.com/news/2026/09/30/new-r1508-part-3-safety-standard-for-industrial-mobile-robot-users-now-available/27186/ and https://www.automationworld.com/factory/robotics/news/55408868/association-for-advancing-automation-new-ansi-standard-completes-industrial-mobile-robot-safety-series
- **ISO 13482:2014** — Safety requirements for personal care robots; the only published international standard aimed at non-industrial service robots. A revision has reached final-draft stage as ISO/FDIS 13482, retitled "Robotics — Safety requirements for service robots" (stage 50.20, FDIS ballot of 8 weeks, listed 15 September 2026). Its scope is service robots in personal and professional/commercial applications and it is not intended to cover industrial or medical applications. Check the edition before quoting a clause. Status: maintained, revision at final-draft stage. Source: https://genorma.com/en/standards/iso-fdis-13482 (FDIS stage, title and scope; iso.org blocks automated fetches) and https://www.iso.org/standard/53820.html (2014 edition)
- **ISO 25785-1 (under development)** — safety requirements for industrial mobile robots with *actively controlled stability*; the scope names quadrupedal, bipedal and wheeled balancing robots, and excludes robots under direct human control. Very early: stage 10.99 "new project approved" as of 9 January 2026, no publication date set — do not plan a conformity route around it. Status: draft. Source: https://genorma.com/en/standards/pren-iso-25785-1

## Regulation and certification routes
- **Regulation (EU) 2023/1230** — the EU Machinery Regulation of 14 June 2023, repealing Machinery Directive 2006/42/EC and Directive 73/361/EEC; applies from 20 January 2027 with deferred application to give operators transition time. Adds cybersecurity, AI-related and lifecycle requirements relative to the old directive. Status: in force, applies 2027. Source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1230/oj
- **Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/2015** — the September 2026 update of the harmonised-standards list under Machinery Directive 2006/42/EC; published in the Official Journal on 7 September 2026, with 69 entries. It cites EN ISO 10218-1:2025 and EN ISO 10218-2:2025, so they now give presumption of conformity under the Directive. Adding a new edition does not automatically or immediately end the legal effect of the previous reference, so check the annex for the cessation date of the 2011 references. Directive references stop having legal effect on 20 January 2027, and the first list of harmonised standards under Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 was still being prepared and expected before the end of 2026 (ibf-solutions item last amended 28 September 2026). Status: in force (Directive); Regulation list pending. Source: https://www.ibf-solutions.com/en/seminars-and-news/news/new-standards-for-industrial-robots-en-iso-10218-1-and-2 and https://safetysoftware.eu/en/blog/harmonised-machinery-standards-september-2026-update and https://www.ibf-solutions.com/en/seminars-and-news/news/new-machinery-regulation-status-of-harmonised-standards
- **UL 3100** — Automated Mobile Platforms (AMPs): battery-operated mobile platforms with or without payload for indoor/outdoor commercial and industrial use; evaluates battery safety, obstacle detection and control fail-safes. Edition 1 dated 2021 with subsequent revisions; a further revision is in circulation. Status: maintained. Source: https://webstore.ansi.org/standards/ul/ul3100ed2021
- **UL 3300** — Service, Communication, Information, Education and Entertainment (SCIEE) robots: public-facing service robots, with test focus on human-robot interaction safety and untrained users including children. Status: maintained. Source: https://www.hardwarecompliance.com/blog/ul-certification-robotics-standard-does-robot-actually-need

## China standards and certification ecosystem
- **GB 11291.1-2011** — 工业环境用机器人 安全要求 第1部分：机器人 (industrial robot safety requirements, Part 1: the robot); published 12 May 2011, effective 1 Oct 2011; replaced GB 11291-1997. Its companion GB 11291.2-2013 (机器人系统与集成, published 17 Dec 2013, effective 1 Nov 2014) covers systems and integration. Mandatory (GB) standards under SAC/TC 159. Status: current, but both track the ISO 10218:2011 generation, not the 2025 edition. Source: https://openstd.samr.gov.cn (national standards registry — search the GB number; deep links are session-bound and the site blocks automated fetches)
- **GB/T 36008-2018** — 机器人与机器人装备 协作机器人 (collaborative robots); published 15 March 2018, effective 1 Oct 2018; corresponds to ISO/TS 15066:2016 and supplements ISO 10218-1:2011 / GB 11291.2-2013. Status: current, recommended (GB/T). Source: https://openstd.samr.gov.cn (registry — search the GB/T number)
- **人形机器人安全要求 第1部分：通用安全 (Humanoid robots — safety requirements — Part 1: general safety)** — national standard plan 20261658-T-604, issued 31 March 2026 with a 12-month project cycle; the committee is the National Robotics Standardization Technical Committee (全国机器人标准化技术委员会) and the executing unit is its Humanoid Robot Subcommittee. The SAMR registry shows it as soliciting comments (正在征求意见). It is a draft, so do not plan a conformity route around it. The Humanoid Robot and Embodied AI Standard System (2026 edition) (《人形机器人与具身智能标准体系（2026版）》) was released on 28 February 2026, and its framework includes a safety and ethics part covering the full lifecycle. Status: draft, in public comment. Source: https://std.samr.gov.cn/gb/search/gbDetailed?id=4E645DFFD63A7395E06397BE0A0AE96A and https://www.news.cn/20260228/c27e2dfdb0f4496494c7e4991f2e8c2f/c.html
- **GB/T 15706-2012** — 机械安全 设计通则 风险评估与风险减小; Chinese counterpart of ISO 12100:2010 (the registry record lists ISO 12100 as a related standard but does not state the adoption degree); published 5 Nov 2012, effective 1 Mar 2013. The Chinese entry point for robot risk assessment. Status: current. Source: https://www.ndls.org.cn/standard/detail/fbdab6808e083e1e0131326e077b0b0e (National Digital Standards Library record: current status, issue and implementation dates)
- **CR certification (中国机器人认证)** — China's unified robot safety, EMC and performance certification scheme run by the China Robot Testing and Certification alliance (机器人检测认证联盟), with core evaluation centres in Shanghai, Shenyang, Guangzhou, Chongqing, Beijing and Wuhu. Covers industrial, service, logistics and collaborative robots plus key components and system integration, and functions as the domestic market credential. Status: regulatory/commercial. Source: https://www.china-tcar.com

## Safety hardware
- **SICK microScan3** — safety laser scanner family, the common choice for AMR protective fields and cell perimeter monitoring. Take the type rating (IEC 61496) and PL from the specific variant's datasheet, not from the family page — they differ across Core/Pro and EFI-pro variants. Status: commercial. Source: https://www.sick.com/us/en/catalog/products/safety/safety-laser-scanners/microscan3/c/g358643 (JavaScript-rendered; open in a browser to read specs)
- **Synapticon safety drives** — servo drives implementing IEC 61800-5-2 functions (STO with hardware blocking of the power stage, SBC, SS1/SS2/SOS); relevant when the safety function has to live in the joint rather than in a cell controller. Status: commercial. Source: https://www.synapticon.com/en/motion-control-academy/safe-torque-off-sto-safe-brake-control-sbc

## Analysis, validation and runtime safety tools
- **SISTEMA (IFA/DGUV)** — free Windows tool that models a safety function's designated architecture and computes the achieved Performance Level per EN ISO 13849-1, with maintained component libraries. Version 3.x corresponds to ISO 13849-1:2023 and version 2.x to the 2015 edition; IFA states a SISTEMA version does not cover multiple standard revisions. Current release 3.0.4 Build 5 (31 October 2025). The de facto evidence artefact for PL verification. Status: maintained, free. Source: https://www.dguv.de/ifa/praxishilfen/praxishilfen-maschinenschutz/software-sistema/index.jsp
- **GTE CoboSafe** — modular force and pressure measurement system for inspecting transient and quasi-static contact on collaborative robots, with spring constants matched to body regions and evaluation software for the biomechanical limits. Status: commercial. Source: https://www.gte.de/en/messtechnik/kraft-druckmesssystem-cobosafe
- **Nav2 Collision Monitor** — open-source ROS 2 node that sits between the velocity controller output and the base, slowing or stopping from raw sensor data independently of costmaps and planners. Genuinely useful risk reduction; not a safety-rated device. Status: maintained. Source: https://docs.nav2.org/rolling/configuration_and_development/configuration_guide/core_servers/collision_monitor/ and https://docs.nav2.org/rolling/tutorials/general_tutorials/using_collision_monitor/using_collision_monitor/ (position in the velocity chain)

## Research (arXiv)
- **Evolution of Safety Requirements in Industrial Robotics: ISO 10218-1/2 (2011 vs. 2025) and Integration of ISO/TS 15066** — clause-level comparative analysis of the structural, terminological and technical changes, including the new robot/collaborative-application classifications and the cybersecurity expansion. The most useful single map of what actually changed. Status: research. arXiv:2602.17822. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17822
- **Safe physical HRI: toward a unified treatment of speed and separation monitoring together with power and force limiting** — deploys both collaborative regimes in one application and measures the performance of transitions between them; KUKA LBR iiwa + RGB-D skeleton tracking. Status: research. arXiv:1908.03046. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.03046
- **Analysis of Deep-Learning Methods in an ISO/TS 15066-Compliant Human-Robot Safety Framework** — compares body recognition, segmentation, pose estimation and body-part segmentation for distance-adaptive speed control; reports up to 15% cycle-time reduction versus conventional safety technology. Status: research. arXiv:2511.19094. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19094
- **A Verification Methodology for Safety Assurance of Robotic Autonomous Systems** — full-lifecycle workflow from hazard analysis and risk assessment through a formal model of the safety controller to runtime verification, demonstrated on an agricultural field robot. Status: research. arXiv:2506.19622. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19622
- **CBF-RL: Safety Filtering Reinforcement Learning in Training with Control Barrier Functions** — enforces barrier constraints during training so the learned policy internalises them, validated on a Unitree G1 humanoid without a runtime filter. Relevant to the "learned policies have no Performance Level" problem. Status: research. arXiv:2510.14959. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14959
- **Toward Certified Functional Safety for Industrial Humanoid Robots: The Fail-Passive Gap and a Feasibility Study** — argues that a legged robot's safe state is actively controlled, which breaks the fail-passive assumption behind ISO 13849-1 / EN 60204-1: removing power from a walking biped causes an uncontrolled fall, so classical de-energisation (Stop Category 0) is itself a hazard. Uses a certified external safety chain (light curtain, e-stop, fail-safe PLC, PROFIsafe) to localise the uncertifiable element to the robot-side reaction chain, and validates the approach on a Unitree G1 EDU pick-and-place cell. It deliberately does not claim end-to-end certified PL e / SIL 3. Status: research. arXiv:2608.02809. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02809
