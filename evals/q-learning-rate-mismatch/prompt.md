expected_outcome: >-
  An expert answer says that the plan is a train/deploy control-rate mismatch and should not be done as framed. An action-chunking policy works in step-indexed time. ACT's data is recorded at a fixed rate (50 Hz on ALOHA), each of the k outputs is a target joint position for a specific number of steps ahead, and the policy is executed "every k steps" in sequence, so chunk length, motion speed, history spacing and the lag between commanded and measured joint state are all tied to the recording step period. Stepping the same checkpoint at 8 Hz when it was recorded at 30 fps stretches every step from about 33 ms to 125 ms. If the arm tracks its targets, motion is about 3.75 times slower, a 100-step chunk spans roughly 12.5 s instead of 3.3 s, and the observation-to-action relationship the network learned (including controller lag and anything moving in the scene) is out of distribution, so success measured this way says little about the policy. The expert also notes that the latency problem is mostly an artifact of querying every step with temporal ensembling. With chunking, inference can be amortised: keep the loop at the recorded rate, execute several actions of each chunk per forward pass or run inference asynchronously so the next chunk is computed while the current one plays, and accept a coarser or no temporal ensemble, or use a smaller or faster model or optimised runtime. If a lower rate is genuinely required, resample the demonstrations to that rate (for example keep every third frame to get 10 Hz), retrain with the chunk size rescaled to the same wall-clock duration, and interpolate commands to the robot's low-level rate, which is what Diffusion Policy does (demonstrations and policy both at 10 Hz, commands linearly interpolated to the UR5's 125 Hz). Primary sources, ACT paper ("Both the teleoperation and data recording happen at 50Hz"; "every k steps, the agent receives an observation, generates the next k actions, and executes the actions in sequence") https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13705 and Diffusion Policy (Appendix D: UR5 accepts commands at 125Hz, "linearly interpolated from the 10Hz command from either human demonstration or the policy"; latency tolerance of up to 4 steps is counted in steps, Fig. 5) https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04137 (both verified 2026-10-02 against the full PDF or HTML text, not only the abstract). The LeRobot async inference guide https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/async documents the decoupled prediction and execution path (actions_per_chunk, chunk_size_threshold). That guide also says to reduce fps if the action queue runs dry; that is a tuning note for the async client and does not claim a checkpoint tolerates a different step period, so an answer that falls back to a lower rate only as a last resort, with resampling and retraining, is still consistent with this outcome.
