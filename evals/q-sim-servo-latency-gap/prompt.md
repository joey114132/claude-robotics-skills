expected_outcome: |
  The planted trap is a wrong diagnosis. The user attributes the real-arm oscillation to dynamics mismatch (mass, friction, contact) and plans to widen randomization and retrain, while the simulation drives an ideal MuJoCo position actuator with no latency. A feedback loop that is stable against an ideal, zero-latency actuator can oscillate when the real chain has delay and limited bandwidth: the policy's command passes over the serial bus and then through the servo's own internal position loop (the STS3215 firmware has P/I/D registers; LeRobot writes P_Coefficient, I_Coefficient and D_Coefficient to every motor). None of this is in the sim, and randomizing link mass or friction cannot reproduce delay or an inner-loop response. An expert answer must (1) say this is an actuation/latency gap and that more mass/friction randomization will not fix it, (2) explain the mechanism (an unmodeled inner position loop plus transport and sensing delay plus quantized targets; the policy was optimized against an ideal actuator and exploits fast response, so it chatters or shakes on a slower plant), (3) recommend measuring it first (step or chirp commands on the real arm, logging commanded vs measured position at the real control rate, or replaying logged real actions open-loop in sim and comparing), then modeling it in sim (first-order or second-order lag plus a delay of a few control steps, deadband/backlash, servo P gain and velocity or acceleration limits matched to the registers actually set, with delay and gain randomized around the identified values), and possibly adding an action-smoothness penalty and a lower-gain or lower-rate command path, then retraining. Source: Hwangbo et al., "Learning agile and dynamic locomotion skills for legged robots" (Science Robotics 2019), https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.08652 , whose ablation found that training with an ideal actuator model (infinite bandwidth, zero latency) or an imperfect analytical one produced violent shaking of the limbs on hardware, attributed to unaccounted delays, and that a learned actuator model closed the gap. Corroborating: Tan et al., "Sim-to-Real: Learning Agile Locomotion For Quadruped Robots" (RSS 2018), https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.10332 , which improved the simulation with system identification, an accurate actuator model and simulated latency. I trained a PPO reaching policy for an SO-101 arm in MuJoCo, with position actuators on every joint, a 2 ms physics step and the policy running at 30 Hz. In sim it hits targets cleanly, but on the real arm with its Feetech STS3215 servos it overshoots and then shakes around the target and never settles. My plan is to add domain randomization on link masses, joint friction and damping (plus or minus 30%), then retrain on 4096 parallel envs and redeploy next week. Is that the right fix, and how should I set the randomization ranges?
