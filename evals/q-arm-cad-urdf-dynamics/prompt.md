expected_outcome: |
  The trap is a unit mismatch, not a tuning problem. The user pasted CAD Mass Properties values into the URDF without converting them. A CAD export in millimetre-gram units reports mass in grams and moments of inertia in g*mm^2, but URDF, Gazebo and Pinocchio read the numbers as SI (kg, kg*m^2), per REP-103. The upper-arm link (a cylinder about 0.3 m long and 40 mm in diameter, motor housing included) was given mass 1840, which the simulator reads as 1840 kg. The pasted numbers are self-consistent as grams and g*mm^2: 1/12*m*L^2 with m=1.84 kg and L=0.3 m is about 0.0138 kg*m^2, which equals 1.4e7 g*mm^2, and 1/2*m*r^2 with r=0.02 m is about 3.7e-4 kg*m^2, which equals 3.7e5 g*mm^2 (1 g*mm^2 = 1e-9 kg*m^2, 1 g = 1e-3 kg). Treating them as SI makes every link roughly a thousand times too heavy and its inertia about 1e9 times too large. That explains the explosion and the gravity torques in the thousands of N*m (m*g*r, with r around 0.15 m, is about 2.7 kN*m for that link alone). An expert answer must say that raising joint damping or shrinking the step only hides the problem, tells the user to convert mass by 1e-3 and inertia by 1e-9 (or re-export in SI), and recommends sanity-checking each link with a hand estimate and checking that the inertia tensor is positive definite and satisfies the triangle inequality. Mentioning that STL meshes exported in mm also need scale="0.001 0.001 0.001" is a plus and a real co-cause of a violent spawn, but it does not replace the inertial unit diagnosis. Second source for SI in Gazebo URDF ("use units of kilograms"): https://classic.gazebosim.org/tutorials?tut=ros_urdf. Source for the SI convention: https://www.ros.org/reps/rep-0103.html (raw text: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ros-infrastructure/rep/master/rep-0103.rst, base units length meter, mass kilogram). Source for the physical-validity conditions on a rotational inertia (non-negative principal moments, triangle inequality): https://raw.githubusercontent.com/RobotLocomotion/drake/master/multibody/tree/rotational_inertia.h

I built a 6-DOF arm URDF by hand from my SolidWorks assembly. For each link I copied the mass and moments of inertia from the Mass Properties dialog into the `<inertial>` tag, and used the exported STL meshes for visual and collision. In Gazebo Harmonic the arm shoots off as soon as I spawn it, and Pinocchio's `computeGeneralizedGravity` gives me joint-2 torques in the thousands of N·m. For example, the upper-arm link (roughly a 40 mm diameter, 0.3 m long cylinder including the motor housing) has mass 1840, ixx=iyy=1.4e7 and izz=3.7e5 in the file. My plan is to raise joint damping and drop the physics step to 0.1 ms to stabilize it, so what damping and step values should I use?
