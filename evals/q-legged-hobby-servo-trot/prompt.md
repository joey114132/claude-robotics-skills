expected_outcome: |
  An expert answer must say that the hardware, not the MPC-vs-RL choice, is the blocking decision. Hobby RC servos take only a position setpoint (a 1-2 ms pulse repeated at a 50 Hz frame rate) and close the loop internally with a potentiometer; there is no torque or current command, no torque or position feedback to the controller by default, and the reduction gearing is high and poorly backdrivable. Locomotion MPC plus whole-body control (the MIT Mini Cheetah recipe) plans ground reaction forces at tens of Hz and maps them to joint torque commands at roughly 500 Hz on top of kHz-rate joint loops, and that recipe relies on highly backdrivable proprioceptive actuators that give open-loop torque control through motor current. Those requirements cannot be met with position-only PWM servos, so MPC plus WBC with push recovery cannot run on this hardware, and a dynamic trot with push recovery is not a realistic target for it (an RL policy would still be limited to servo position targets with unmodeled servo dynamics). The recommendation must change: either move to torque-controllable low-gear-ratio (quasi-direct-drive or proprioceptive) actuators before investing in MPC or RL, or scope the current robot down to a slow, statically stable, position-controlled gait (kinematic gait generator plus inverse kinematics, IMU-based posture correction), keeping simulation and a gantry in the plan. Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.06586 (Kim, Di Carlo, Katz, Bledt, Kim, WBIC + MPC on Mini Cheetah, fetched and confirmed 2026-10-02: WBC computes joint torque, position and velocity commands, proprioceptive actuators that are highly backdrivable with open-loop torque control via motor current, MPC 30 Hz and WBC 500 Hz, joint PD 40 kHz) and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Servo_(radio_control) (50 Hz PWM position command, potentiometer loop, most hobby servos do not report back).

I'm building a 12-DOF quadruped (3 joints per leg, about 2.5 kg total) with 20 kg·cm metal-gear RC servos (MG996R class) driven by a PCA9685 from a Raspberry Pi 5, plus a BNO085 IMU. The goal is a dynamic trot at around 0.5 m/s that can take a shove without falling. Should I implement centroidal MPC with a whole-body controller like the MIT Mini Cheetah, or train an RL locomotion policy in Isaac Lab and deploy that? Which one would you go with?
