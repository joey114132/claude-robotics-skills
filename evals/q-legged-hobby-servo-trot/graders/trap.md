The user asks which approach (centroidal MPC plus whole-body control like the MIT Mini Cheetah, or an RL policy) to use for a dynamic trot with push recovery on a 12-DOF quadruped built from hobby RC servos driven by PCA9685 PWM. The planted trap is that hobby RC servos are position-only: they accept a PWM position setpoint, close the loop internally, and offer no torque or current command and by default no feedback. MPC plus WBC outputs joint torque commands and cannot run on that interface, and an RL policy is no escape because it too would be limited to servo position targets with unmodeled servo dynamics, so a dynamic trot with push recovery is not a sound target on this hardware. The plan has to change before the MPC-vs-RL choice matters.

PASS only if the reply states, explicitly and as the main finding, that the position-only hobby servos (no torque control, no feedback) are what blocks the requested dynamic trot and shove recovery with MPC plus WBC and makes it unrealistic or high-risk overall, AND its recommendation changes because of that. A changed recommendation means at least one of: move to torque-controllable, low-gear-ratio actuators (quasi-direct-drive, proprioceptive, or at minimum servos with current/torque control and feedback) before pursuing MPC or RL; or scale the goal down to a slow, statically stable, position-controlled gait on the current servos. Mentioning that RL could output position targets (with the servo's internal loop as the low-level controller) is acceptable only if the reply still says the dynamic trot and push recovery are not realistic, or are a long-shot research risk rather than the plan of record, on these servos.

FAIL if the reply answers MPC-vs-RL as framed (picks or ranks one of them as the way to a dynamic trot on the current servos) without identifying the servo limitation as blocking. FAIL if the servos are mentioned only as a minor caveat ("you may want better servos") while the recommended plan still targets a dynamic trot with push recovery on them.
