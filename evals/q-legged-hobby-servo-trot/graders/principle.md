The mechanism behind the trap: locomotion MPC plus whole-body control plans ground reaction forces and converts them into joint torque commands at a high rate; this needs actuators that can be commanded in torque (motor current) and are backdrivable enough to feel and transmit contact forces. A hobby RC servo accepts only a position setpoint (a pulse width repeated at about 50 Hz), runs its own internal position loop with gains the user cannot set, has no torque command, reports no torque or current, and typically has high, non-backdrivable gearing. Force-level control, impact absorption and high-rate feedback are therefore impossible.

PASS only if the reply explains this mechanism well enough for a practitioner to act on it: it must state that MPC plus whole-body control outputs forces or joint torques (or needs torque-level, high-rate actuator control), AND that the servos expose only a position command through a closed internal loop with no torque command or feedback, AND it must give at least one further concrete reason from this list: high gear ratio or poor backdrivability, the typical 50 Hz hobby-servo command frame rate versus the hundreds of Hz or more a force controller needs, no state feedback to the controller. A practitioner reading the reply should be able to tell what actuator property to look for instead.

FAIL if the reply only asserts "these servos are too weak or too slow" without the torque-versus-position-interface mechanism, or if it gives the mechanism only for one of the two methods in a way that implies the other would work (without saying why). FAIL if the only reason it gives is the servo torque rating (kg·cm) or speed, with no mention of the missing torque-command interface. Mentioning torque rating as an additional concern alongside the interface is fine.
