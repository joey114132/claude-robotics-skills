expected_outcome: >-
  An expert answer says that the plan cannot give repeatable force, because in plain Position Control mode (Operating Mode 3) the servo regulates position, not contact force. Once the pads touch the berry, the torque is roughly the P gain times the remaining position error, so the force depends on how stiff the berry is, where contact happens, gear backlash and compliance, supply voltage and temperature, none of which the "ticks past contact" number captures. Tuning the P gain or the overshoot against a kitchen scale will therefore give a number that works for one berry on one day. The expert recommends moving the force limit into the torque loop. On Dynamixel X-series servos such as the XL330-M288 that means Current-based Position Control (Operating Mode 5), where Goal Current sets a limit on the calculated desired current, or pure Current Control (Mode 0), which the manual calls ideal for a gripper. The current is calibrated against measured pad force with the scale, because transmission friction and finger geometry change the current-to-force relation. Force or tactile sensing is the option when the force must be known or closed-loop. The expert may also note that in Mode 3 the PID output is only capped by the PWM limit, so a position command past contact keeps driving the motor against the berry (the manual's Overload Error covers persistent load above maximum output). Primary source, Dynamixel e-Manual for the XL330-M288 (Operating Mode and Goal Current entries) https://emanual.robotis.com/docs/en/dxl/x/xl330-m288/ and for the XM430-W350 https://emanual.robotis.com/docs/en/dxl/x/xm430-w350/ (verified 2026-10-02: Mode 0 is "ideal for a gripper"; Mode 5 Goal Current "decides the final desired current by setting a limit on the calculated desired current"; Mode 3 PID output is limited only by Goal PWM)

I'm building a two-finger strawberry gripper around a single Dynamixel XL330-M288, driven from Python with the DynamixelSDK in its default Position Control mode. The goal is to hold each berry at roughly 1.5 N without bruising it. My plan is to close until the pads touch, then command a goal position a fixed number of ticks past the contact point, and tune the position P gain until a kitchen scale under the pad reads about 1.5 N. How should I pick the overshoot tick count and P gain so the force comes out repeatable from berry to berry?
